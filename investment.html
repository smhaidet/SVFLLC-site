Capital partnerships

Invest alongside an experienced developer.

SVF is seeking relationships with private investors, family offices and strategic capital partners for select Puerto Rico hospitality and real-estate development opportunities. Every opportunity begins with downside analysis, disciplined underwriting and direct sponsor involvement.

1. Understand the market

Study actual competitive occupancy, room rates, customer demand and existing supply.

2. Buy correctly

Value is created at acquisition. The starting basis must support development cost, contingency and realistic operations.

3. Underwrite conservatively

Use realistic occupancy assumptions, meaningful construction contingencies and time-overrun allowances.

4. Plan the downside

Understand the exit strategy and Plan B before committing investor capital.

5. Execute

Once the research and underwriting justify the opportunity, disciplined execution becomes the priority.

6. Communicate

Investors should understand the opportunity, risks, progress and decisions being made throughout the project.

Investor FAQ

What capital partners should expect.

What does SVF invest in?

SVF focuses primarily on hospitality and value-add real estate where acquisition basis, market demand, development potential and operating economics create a clear path to value creation.

How involved is the sponsor?

SVF maintains direct senior-level involvement in sourcing, underwriting, structuring, development oversight and major operating decisions. Capital partners are not passed to a junior acquisition team after funding.

How is risk evaluated?

Projects are underwritten with conservative operating assumptions, meaningful construction contingencies, downside scenarios and a defined Plan B before capital is committed.

How are investors kept informed?

SVF's preferred approach is regular project-level reporting covering budget versus actual, construction progress, schedule, material risks, decisions and next steps.

Does SVF use outside capital?

SVF's underlying track record includes sponsor capital, private investor equity and bank financing. Outside capital is treated as a fiduciary-level responsibility requiring clear disclosure, disciplined execution and direct accountability.
