Investment approach

Development through disciplined underwriting.

SVF approaches every project from the downside first. The objective is not to build projections around the best possible outcome, but to understand what can go wrong, price those risks honestly and determine whether the project remains attractive under conservative assumptions.

1. Understand the market

Study actual competitive occupancy, room rates, customer demand and existing supply.

2. Buy correctly

Value is created at acquisition. The starting basis must support development cost, contingency and realistic operations.

3. Underwrite conservatively

Use realistic occupancy assumptions, meaningful construction contingencies and time-overrun allowances.

4. Plan the downside

Understand the exit strategy and Plan B before committing investor capital.

5. Execute

Once the research and underwriting justify the opportunity, disciplined execution becomes the priority.

6. Communicate

Investors should understand the opportunity, risks, progress and decisions being made throughout the project.

Investor alignment

Passive capital. Active transparency.

Marcus believes outside capital carries an especially high duty of care. Investors should understand both the opportunity and the risk before committing capital.

SVF’s preferred investment relationships are with steady, long-term investors seeking passive participation in professionally developed real-estate assets. The project sponsor remains directly involved in sourcing, underwriting, structuring and overseeing the project while providing consistent reporting on budget, schedule, risks and next steps.

Long-term perspective

Build durable assets. Create current income. Compound equity over time.

SVF is focused on developing assets capable of supporting long-term ownership and generational wealth rather than rapid property turnover.
